03 / Things I have explored

My digital discoveries.

These are five tools I explored and considered from a teacher's point of view. Each one offers a different way to support English language learning.

DISCOVERY 01

LyricsTraining

Listening · Vocabulary · Spelling

LyricsTraining uses music videos and song lyrics to help learners practise listening. Students listen and fill in missing words, giving them exposure to pronunciation, rhythm, intonation and everyday expressions.

  • For teachers: offers authentic listening material and opportunities to teach vocabulary and pronunciation.
  • For students: makes listening practice enjoyable and encourages repeated listening.
My classroom idea & how to use it
Listen, identify and complete

Choose an age-appropriate song related to the lesson. Students listen and fill in missing lyrics, check their answers, discuss unfamiliar words and write original sentences using selected vocabulary.

  1. Visit LyricsTraining.
  2. Choose a suitable song and exercise mode.
  3. Set an appropriate difficulty level.
  4. Ask students to listen and complete the lyrics.
  5. Review answers, pronunciation and vocabulary together.

My evaluation: Music can motivate learners and expose them to authentic language. However, fast lyrics, slang and unclear pronunciation may challenge beginners. Teachers should preview songs and choose content carefully.

DISCOVERY 02

Educaplay

Vocabulary · Grammar · Reading

Educaplay lets teachers create interactive activities such as quizzes, crosswords, word searches, matching games, fill-in-the-blanks and dictation exercises.

  • For teachers: offers different activity formats and ways to share exercises and review available results.
  • For students: provides varied practice and makes revision more interactive.
My classroom idea & how to use it
Vocabulary discovery challenge

Create a word search or crossword about environmental issues. Students complete it individually or in pairs, discuss unfamiliar words and write sentences using the new vocabulary.

  1. Visit Educaplay and sign in.
  2. Create a new activity and select a format.
  3. Add a title, instructions, questions and answers.
  4. Preview and publish the activity.
  5. Share its link or QR code with students.
  6. Review available reports and give feedback.

My evaluation: Its variety is useful for practising different language areas. However, some activities may focus more on speed than understanding, and not every format develops speaking or extended writing. Some features are plan-dependent.

DISCOVERY 03

Baamboozle

Speaking · Teamwork · Vocabulary

Baamboozle is a classroom game platform where students work in teams to answer questions. Teachers can use existing games or create their own question sets, then display the game on a shared screen.

  • For teachers: makes it easier to organise team revision and speaking activities.
  • For students: encourages cooperation, discussion and confidence.
My classroom idea & how to use it
Vocabulary team challenge

Divide students into teams and display questions about occupations. Teams take turns choosing tiles, discussing answers and using new vocabulary in spoken sentences.

  1. Visit Baamboozle.
  2. Explore the game library or create a game.
  3. Add questions and answers.
  4. Save and open the game.
  5. Display it on a shared classroom screen.
  6. Organise teams and facilitate discussion.

My evaluation: The shared-screen format is practical when students do not have individual devices. However, confident students may dominate, and group answers do not show each learner's individual ability. Teachers should rotate turns and involve everyone.

DISCOVERY 04

Blooket

Gamification · Grammar · Engagement

Blooket combines educational questions with different game modes. Teachers can create or select question sets and host activities in which students answer questions while playing.

  • For teachers: provides flexible revision activities and reports for reviewing performance.
  • For students: makes practice more engaging and offers repeated opportunities to recall information.
My classroom idea & how to use it
English vocabulary adventure

Prepare questions about food and nutrition. Students join using a game code, answer questions and take part in the selected game. End with a discussion of unfamiliar vocabulary.

  1. Visit Blooket and create a teacher account.
  2. Create a question set and add answer options.
  3. Save the set and select an available game mode.
  4. Host the game and share the joining code.
  5. Review results and discuss common errors.

My evaluation: Different game modes can maintain student interest. However, competition and luck can affect scores, and learners may focus on gameplay instead of language learning. Scores should not be the only measure of understanding.

DISCOVERY 05

Gimkit

Retrieval practice · Vocabulary · Grammar

Gimkit is a quiz-based learning platform. Teachers create question sets called Kits. Students answer questions in different game modes and, depending on the mode, earn virtual money that can be used for upgrades or power-ups.

  • For teachers: supports revision, custom questions and review of available performance reports.
  • For students: encourages repeated practice, recall and strategic thinking.
My classroom idea & how to use it
Grammar and vocabulary challenge

Create a Kit on subject-verb agreement. Students join with a game code, answer questions and earn virtual money. Afterwards, discuss common mistakes and explain why each answer is correct.

  1. Visit Gimkit and create an educator account.
  2. Create a new Kit and add questions and answers.
  3. Save the Kit and select an available game mode.
  4. Host the game and share the joining code.
  5. Review results and provide feedback.

My evaluation: Its virtual economy adds a strategic element and encourages repeated question practice. However, rewards can distract from learning, and fast-paced activities may pressure slower readers. Some modes and features require a paid plan.

04 / What I have learned

A personal reflection.

Exploring these digital tools has helped me see how varied and creative language learning can be. Each platform offers something different: music can make listening practice more enjoyable, interactive activities can reinforce vocabulary and grammar, and team games can create opportunities for communication and collaboration.

What I find important is not simply using technology because it is available, but understanding why a particular tool is suitable for a lesson. A teacher needs to consider students' language levels, learning objectives, access to devices and internet connection, and the kind of support learners need.

I also realise that a fun activity is not automatically an effective lesson. Students still need clear instructions, meaningful practice and feedback. Digital tools should support the teacher's purpose, not replace the teacher's role.

As I continue my journey in education, I hope to become more confident in choosing and adapting digital resources. I want to create lessons where students feel comfortable participating, making mistakes and improving their English step by step.

“Keep learning, keep creating, and keep becoming.”

This is what IMOON represents to me: a personal space to document progress, reflect on new ideas and grow into the educator I aspire to be.
